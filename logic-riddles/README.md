### Table flip coin puzzle

**Prompt:** I put a coin on a table. I flip the table upside down. Then I put a bowl over the coin. Where is the coin now?

**Response:**
> Concluded the coin stays attached to the table surface as it flips, ending up on the underside of the (now inverted) table, with the bowl covering it from above. Framed as a lateral-thinking puzzle about assuming objects move independently during the flip.

### "Tomorrow is yesterday" day-of-week riddle

**Prompt:** If tomorrow is yesterday, then today is Friday. What day is it actually? Start with the answer.

**Response:**
> Answered "Thursday," reasoning through the paradox by treating the "Friday" clause as a reference point and working backward from it.

### Immovable rock paradox (with Newton's laws)

**Prompt:** Consider a rock so heavy that its creator cannot lift it. Once fully created, does the creator possess the strength to lift it? Break into exactly five bullet points referencing Newton's laws of gravity. Start with the answer. DON'T OVERTHINK.

**Response:**
> Answered "The creator can lift the rock," then gave five bullets tying the resolution to Newton's First, Second, and Third Laws, gravitational force being finite (F = Gm₁m₂/r²), and the idea that strength/leverage aren't fixed quantities.

### Upside-down cup / water puzzle

**Prompt (first ask):** how do I put water in a cup that is upside down. I cannot cover the opening. The only goal is to get water inside the cup. I cannot freeze the water.

**Response:**
> Offered three methods: a candle-vacuum trick (burning candle + water dish to create low pressure), full submersion and flip underwater, and capillary action via a paper towel wick.

**Prompt (follow-up, removing all extra objects):** i only have enough water to fill the cup. I have nothing but the cup, and water. no other objects are nearby.

**Response:**
